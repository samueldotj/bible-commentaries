## Background: the English translations

The fathers' own texts are ancient. What matters is the date of each **English translation**.

- Many quotations name 19th-century translations that are in the public domain: the Ante-Nicene and
  Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, and Newman's 1841 translation of the *Catena Aurea*.
- Others come from works whose only published English translations are modern and may still be under
  copyright. Examples: Oecumenius on Revelation and Acts, Ephrem on Hebrews, Ambrosiaster, Theodoret on
  Paul, Bede on Samuel and Proverbs.
- The compiler may have made new translations of some works. If so, the "Public domain" statement would
  be the compiler's own grant, and it should be confirmed with SermonIndex.

Each quotation names its author and source work (`<i>— Tractates on John 11</i>`). That makes it
possible to keep only works whose translation is known to be in the public domain.

## What is in it

- 18,656 verse entries. Every book is covered, but the Old Testament history books thinly: 1–2
  Chronicles 2–3% of verses, 2 Samuel 12%.
- 13,415,238 words of quotation, plus 582,875 words of author and source lines.
- 4% of the paragraph text repeats across verses.
- The longest entry is `JHN.1.1` at 33,967 words.
- 271 authors. The most quoted: Augustine (17,655 quotations), Chrysostom (14,831), Bede (14,041), Jerome
  (13,229) and Origen (6,994).
- 717,493 words (about 5%) come through Aquinas's *Catena Aurea* ("as quoted by Aquinas, AD 1274").
- Each quotation is `<p><b>Author</b>: text<br/><i>— Source work</i></p>`. The unit of translation should
  be the quotation, with the author's name and source kept.
